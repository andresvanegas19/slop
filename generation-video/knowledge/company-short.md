# Company short (brand story) structure

## The who, what, why, call-to-action arc
A company short answers four questions in order. Who we are (the people or place), what we do (the work shown in action), why it matters (the impact on customers or the community), then one call to action. Each beat gets at least one scene. The arc works at every length; longer versions add proof and people scenes, not more claims.

## Authentic people and places
Brand videos feel trustworthy when they show real-looking people in real workplaces: hands at work, candid collaboration, customers being helped. Prefer natural light, documentary framing and genuine expressions over staged stock-photo poses. Show specific details of the work (tools, materials, the product being made) instead of generic office scenes.

## One memorable line
Build the short around a single line the viewer will remember, a plain statement of purpose ("We make mornings easier for small cafés"). Put it in the narration of the "why" scene or the final scene, and make the other lines lead up to it. Avoid jargon, buzzwords and mission-statement filler.

## Consistent look across scenes
Use one visual style for the whole short: the same color grade, lighting mood, lens feel and level of realism. Consistency makes a sequence of generated stills feel like one film and makes the brand feel coherent. Keep images free of text and logos, and put the company name in the on-screen headline or narration.

## Pacing for company shorts
5s: who we are, then the call to action. 10s: who, what, why, CTA (about 2–3s each). 15s: who, what, why, proof, CTA. 30s: who, what we do (two angles), why, proof, our people, CTA (about 4–5s each). Open with a warm, inviting image, let the middle breathe with slow camera moves, and give the final scene about 20% of the runtime.

## Call to action for brand videos
End with one gentle, clear invitation that fits the company: visit, get in touch, join the team, or discover the product. The final frame should feel welcoming (open doors, a smiling team, the storefront) and hold long enough to read. Narration stays at about 2.5 words per second in a sincere, human tone, speaking as "we" to "you".
