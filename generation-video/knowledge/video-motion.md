# Motion prompts for short clips (3–5 s)

## One clear action
A 3–5 second clip has room for one action, e.g. "the hen turns its head toward the camera" or "coins spill out of the price tag". Multiple sequential actions get rushed, merged or dropped.

## Start the action immediately
Describe motion that begins at the first frame. Avoid slow setups ("after a while", "eventually"): the clip ends before they happen. Use present-tense verbs.

## Camera motion vocabulary
Name the camera move explicitly and keep it gentle: slow push in, slow pull out / zoom out, pan left / pan right, tilt up, orbit around the subject, static camera / locked-off shot, subtle handheld drift. Pick one camera move per clip. "Static camera" is a good choice when the subject's action should be the focus.

## No cuts inside a clip
A short clip is one continuous shot. Don't ask for a scene change, a cut, a new location or a transition inside it; transitions happen between clips in the edit (e.g. a 0.3 s crossfade).

## Stay consistent with the keyframe
The clip starts from the frame image, so describe motion of what is already there. Don't introduce new main subjects or change the style, palette or lighting mid-clip.

## Pacing
Keep motion calm and readable: slow, smooth, continuous. Fast or chaotic motion causes warping in short generations. Match energy to the narration — a big reveal can use a push in, a quiet beat a slow drift or static shot.
