# Cinematic storytelling for generated video

## Shot anatomy
Write every shot as one real moment, filmed on a phone, that is about to move. In order: the subject with a concrete action and a readable emotion (a woman laughs as she pulls her collar up against the wind); the setting with time of day and weather (a cliff path beside a white lighthouse, late afternoon, light sea breeze); the available light (low golden-hour sun behind them, warm glow on hair); the framing (phone camera at eye level, intimate medium shot, natural background blur); exactly one phone-like camera move; the phone-filmed look. One subject, one action, one idea per shot.

## Lighting vocabulary
Golden-hour backlight: low warm sun behind the subject, glowing edges, lens flare, long shadows. Rim light: a thin bright outline separating subject from background. Blue hour: soft cool ambient light just after sunset, warm practicals glowing. Overcast soft light: even, gentle, flattering skin. Window light: soft directional side light indoors. Practicals: visible lamps, string lights, candles, a café's warm interior light, which motivate the light in the frame. Say where the light comes from and what it touches.

## Lens and depth of field
The house look is a phone's main camera (roughly a 26mm-equivalent view) held at eye or chest height. Get close: intimate medium and close shots of faces, hands and small gestures, with the natural background blur of a phone's portrait-like depth of field. A wider medium shot establishes the place; then move in. Eye level feels honest; a slightly low angle while walking feels alive.

## Camera moves: one per shot
Pick exactly one phone-like move and name it plainly: walking follow (moving with someone), slow push in by hand (intimacy, realization), quick reframe (catching a reaction), over-the-shoulder (seeing what they see), handheld pan (revealing the place), or a handheld hold with gentle sway (let the moment play). Two moves in one short shot read as chaos. Motion stays motivated by the people.

## The phone-filmed look
Describe it as how the footage was captured, not as quality tags: handheld smartphone footage, slight natural sway and micro-jitter, available natural light, phone-lens shallow depth of field, true-to-life color with gentle warmth, candid and unposed. It should feel like someone who was there filmed it. Avoid "8k, ultra detailed, masterpiece", studio lighting, glossy product renders and oversaturated HDR. Keep the same look in every shot.

## Story beats by length
5 seconds: one shot, one moment, a hook that resolves (the door opens, the steam rises, they look up and smile). 10 seconds: hook → build → payoff, three or four shots: an establishing image, a human action, a small emotional payoff, a warm closing image. 15 seconds: hook, two build shots that show the work or the place from different angles, payoff, close. 30 seconds: hook, context, three to four build shots with rising emotion, a turning moment, payoff, closing image. The payoff is a feeling (relief, delight, belonging), not a slogan.

## Continuity across shots
Decide once, then repeat in every shot: who appears (age, hair, wardrobe with colors), the location and its landmarks, the time of day and weather, the light direction, and the color grade. Move the camera between shots (wide, medium, close) but not the world. Light that comes from the left stays on the left. The same sweater, the same sky, the same palette.

## Experience, not presentation
A presentation shows slides with words; an experience lets the viewer be there, as if they were holding the phone. No text slides and no captions over the images. Show, don't tell: turn a claim ("we care about our guests") into a visible moment (the owner hands a warm cup to a regular and they both laugh). Favor candid human moments with genuine emotion — a laugh, a glance, a hand on a shoulder — hands at work, natural light, weather and small imperfections. Moments over products. Let ambient sound (wind, waves, cups, footsteps) carry the scene; the narration sits on top, sparingly.

## Describe what you want (no negative prompts)
FLUX has no negative prompt. Listing unwanted things ("no blur, no extra fingers, no people") can pull them in. Describe the desired result instead: "clear sky" rather than "no clouds", "an empty beach at dawn" rather than "no people", "clean unbranded surfaces" rather than listing logos to avoid. Keep one short line that the frame has no visible text, and never quote words that should not appear.
