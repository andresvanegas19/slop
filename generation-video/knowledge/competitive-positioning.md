# Competitive positioning ads

## Differentiate without naming competitors
A competitive ad shows why the company is the better choice without ever naming, showing or hinting at a specific competitor: no names, logos, domains, product names or look-alike packaging. Say what the company does differently ("brewed in town, delivered the same day") instead of what others do wrong. Naming rivals invites legal risk, spends the viewer's attention on someone else's brand and dates the ad. Competitor research only decides which strengths to lead with.

## Lead with the problem buyers already feel
Open on the frustration every buyer in the category knows (waiting, hidden fees, generic products, slow support) shown in one clear frame. Keep it about the viewer's situation, not about a named alternative, so the ad works for anyone who has shopped around. One problem is enough; the rest of the ad answers it.

## Our approach, then the one thing only we do
After the problem, show how the company approaches it in a single image, then give the differentiator its own scene: the capability, promise or detail the competitors' own pages do not claim. Pick the strongest one and make it concrete (a number, a place, a guarantee, a person), not a vague superlative like "the best" or "better than the rest".

## Proof beats claims
Back the differentiator with one piece of evidence from the company's own site: a customer count, years in business, a certification, a quote or a guarantee. Use the exact fact the research found and show it plainly (a real setting, a real person, a readable headline). Never invent statistics or compare numbers with a named rival.

## Switching call to action
End with one low-friction action that makes switching feel easy: "Try it free", "Book a demo", "Order your first box". Give the CTA its own calm final scene with the product or service as the hero, and avoid "switch from X" wording that names the alternative.
