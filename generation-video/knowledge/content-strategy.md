# Content strategy playbook

## Audience first, brand as the bridge
Content never starts from what the brand wants to say. Start from what the audience needs to solve, is searching for, or is curious about, and let the brand appear only as the bridge to that answer. Test every script: if a scene only makes sense to the company, rewrite it from the viewer's side. Narrow the audience to one specific sub-niche instead of the whole market.

## Hook: the dopamine ladder in the first 0.6 to 2 seconds
Attention is won or lost in the first 0.6 to 2 seconds. Stage one is stimulation, a visual taser: sudden motion, an extreme close-up, an unexpected angle or a strong light or color contrast. Stage two is captivation, a curiosity loop: an open question or promise the viewer needs closed, and the video only closes it at the end. Never open on a logo, a slow establishing shot or the brand introducing itself.

## Multihooks: picture, sound and words at once
The strongest openings stack hooks on every channel at the same moment: a visual jolt in the frame, a sound that belongs to the action, and a short spoken or on-screen line that opens the loop. Each channel should carry the same idea, so the hook lands even with the sound off or the eyes half on the screen.

## The 5-7-10 content matrix
Scale content without repeating yourself by combining 5 sub-niches of the audience, 7 psychological angles and 10 visual formats. Angles: the myth (a belief proved wrong), the versus (old way against new way, never naming another company), the quick hack (one fast, specific win), the common mistake and its fix, the transformation from before to after, behind the scenes (the real process and a small setback), and the contrarian truth. Formats rotate between first-person POV, day in the life, hands-only process close-ups, before and after, candid reaction, walk-and-talk, observational mini-documentary, slow-motion detail, unexpected angles and the same routine repeated with one change. Novelty comes from new combinations, not new topics.

## Interest ratio over volume
The algorithm no longer rewards posting more; it rewards the interest ratio and retention. About 10% of well-thought-out content produces 90% of the results, so spend the effort on fewer pieces with a stronger hook, a clearer value shift and a single idea, and cut anything that does not hold attention.

## The three-phase content funnel
Split publishing into three jobs. Growth attracts new viewers with high value or entertainment while the brand stays in the background. Connection builds trust through vulnerability and the story of the process: the people, the effort, the small failures along the way. Sale answers the main objection with proof and ends with one clear call to action. Ads usually serve the sale stage and company shorts the connection stage.

## Building a brand community
Lasting sales come from a brand community, not from single videos. It grows around a visible founder, a counter-current message that polarizes and gives people a side to join, internal codes and rituals the community repeats, and authority shown by documenting results and failures in real time instead of only showing finished wins.

## Value shift: every scene is a narrative event
Every scene, act or piece of content must produce a narrative event: it turns a universal value of human experience through conflict, such as strength to weakness, doubt to confidence, lie to truth, or alone to together. Name the value at the start of the scene and at the end; if it did not change and the scene only explains, cut it or rewrite it around a small conflict.

## Visual identity and production design
The visual world translates the director's vision and supports the story. Define it before shooting: sets, wardrobe, props and one color palette, and keep them identical across scenes so the piece reads as one film. Document the look in a moodboard (references and mood) and a lookbook (the concrete palette, wardrobe, props, light and framing rules) that every scene prompt follows.

## The pitch: selling the project
To get an idea funded or approved, structure a short, time-boxed pitch. Make the premise tangible in one sentence and one striking image, show clear audiovisual references (moodboard, lookbook, sample frames), and prove budget and technical viability: what it costs, how long it takes, and which tools and people make it possible.
